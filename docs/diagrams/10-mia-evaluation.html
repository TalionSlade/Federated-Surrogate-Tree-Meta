Mathematical Foundation
MIA is a Neyman-Pearson hypothesis test
▌ WHAT — Yeom 2018 score function
For row $(x,y)$, the adversary computes a score and thresholds it. Bounded by DP & the trade-off curve:
$\mathrm{score}(x,y) = \mathrm{surrogate.predict\_proba}(x)[y]$
$\begin{aligned} H_0 &:\ (x,y) \notin \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}} \\ H_1 &:\ (x,y) \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}} \\ \hat{H} &= H_1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{score} > \tau \end{aligned}$
$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{TPR}(\tau) &= \Pr[\mathrm{score} > \tau \mid H_1] \\ \mathrm{FPR}(\tau) &= \Pr[\mathrm{score} > \tau \mid H_0] \\ \mathrm{adv} &= \max_{\tau}(\mathrm{TPR} - \mathrm{FPR}) \\ \mathrm{AUC} &= \int \mathrm{TPR}\,d(\mathrm{FPR}) \end{aligned}$
$\mathrm{TPR} \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\cdot \mathrm{FPR} + \delta$
# Tight curve: Kairouz-Oh-Viswanath 2015
▌ WHY — empirics ≠ analytics
The analytic (ε, δ)-DP bound is a worst-case over all adversaries. Real adversaries are weaker — the Yeom score is just one of many possible tests.

An empirical MIA tells you how much of the worst case the real attack achieves. For small ε the gap is huge: the analytic bound says "could leak", the empirical attack says "doesn't leak in practice".

Reporting both is the gold standard for a privacy paper. The first is sound; the second is informative.
▌ MINDSET — TPR@low-FPR matters more than AUC
AUC is the wrong headline. An adversary cares about a specific operating point — e.g. "if I'm willing to flag 1% of non-members as members, how many real members do I catch?"

The framework reports TPR@1%FPR by default. This is the same metric used by Carlini et al. 2022 — the modern privacy-leak yardstick.

An attack that beats random by 1pp on AUC may still catch zero rows at 1%FPR. Don't be misled by the average; pay attention to the low-FPR regime.

Trade-off metrics
\(\mathrm{TPR}(\tau)\)
True-positive rate at threshold \(\tau\) — fraction of true members flagged as members.
tpr = (scores_member > tau).mean().
\(\mathrm{FPR}(\tau)\)
False-positive rate — fraction of non-members wrongly flagged as members.
fpr = (scores_nonmember > tau).mean().
\(\mathrm{adv}\)
Yeom advantage — \(\max_\tau (\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR})\). The single best operating point.
MIAResult.advantage = (tpr - fpr).max().
\(\mathrm{AUC}\)
Area under the ROC curve — global discriminative ability across all thresholds.
sklearn.metrics.roc_auc_score(labels, scores) → MIAResult.auc.
\(\mathrm{TPR@1\%FPR}\)
Low-FPR operating point (Carlini 2022 yardstick) — TPR at FPR \(\le 0.01\).
MIAResult.tpr_at_1pct_fpr; the framework's default headline metric.
DP bound on the attack
\(\varepsilon,\ \delta\)
The very same DP parameters used to noise the soft labels — set the upper bound on TPR.
cfg.dp.target_epsilon / target_delta; reported alongside MIA results.
\(\mathrm{TPR}\le e^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{FPR}+\delta\)
Kairouz-Oh-Viswanath trade-off bound — the analytic worst case for any adversary.
